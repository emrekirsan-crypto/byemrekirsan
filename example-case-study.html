Example case study

Orders that stopped getting lost

A neighbourhood bakery took wholesale orders from cafés over WhatsApp. Orders got lost between messages. Now they arrive in one list, every evening at the same time.

ⓘThis is an illustrative example. It shows how I document projects. The bakery and the numbers are fictional. Real case studies will appear here as projects are completed.

Client
Bakery, 6 people (fictional)
My role
Observation, process map, build
Duration

Built with

01

The problem

Twelve cafés ordered bread for the next morning through WhatsApp messages, voice notes and phone calls. Every night, the owner scrolled through all of it and copied the orders onto paper.

It took close to an hour every night. Some orders were missed, some were baked twice, and about two deliveries a week went wrong.

02

The map

I spent two evenings and one early morning at the bakery, timing each step from the first message to the last delivery. The baking itself was fine. The problem was one step before it.

Café sends a message
Owner reads at night
Copies onto paper
Bakers bake
Driver delivers

Copying onto paper: 50 to 60 minutes every night, and the step where almost every mistake started.

03

The system

Instead of a big app, a short order form each café opens on its phone. Its usual order is already filled in. Everything lands in one list that closes at 21:00 and prints as a baking sheet.

  • Saved usual orders: a regular order takes about 20 seconds
  • Cut-off at 21:00, so the list is final before baking starts
  • One printed sheet for the bakers, one route list for the driver
